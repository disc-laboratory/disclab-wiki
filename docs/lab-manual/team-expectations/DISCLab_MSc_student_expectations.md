# MSc Student Expectations and Working Principles

This document outlines the main expectations, working norms, and professional responsibilities associated with conducting an MSc research project in the **Digital Intelligence for Structures and Construction** research group at NTU Singapore. It is intended to promote transparency, alignment, and a productive working relationship throughout the student's period in the group.

## Purpose of this Document

This document is provided at the beginning of the MSc research project to clarify expectations from the outset. Its purpose is to reduce ambiguity, support mutual understanding, and help the student make effective use of the comparatively limited period available for completing the research and dissertation.

This document is **not an employment contract** and does not replace university, school, degree-programme, scholarship, funding-agency, safety, ethics, or other institutional rules. Official NTU policies, MSc programme requirements, and formal academic regulations remain controlling where applicable. Instead, this document sets out the **research-group-level expectations** that the MSc student is expected to understand and consider when undertaking research within the group.

## Nature of the MSc Research Project

An MSc research project is both an academic training experience and an opportunity to conduct a focused piece of research leading to a dissertation. Because the available period is substantially shorter than a PhD, the project normally has a more clearly defined scope and requires disciplined planning from an early stage.

The MSc student is not expected to operate at the level of independence of a PhD student or postdoctoral researcher. However, the student is expected to progressively develop ownership of the assigned project, demonstrate initiative, engage critically with the research problem, and become increasingly capable of conducting the work with appropriate independence.

The MSc student is expected to:
- understand and progressively take ownership of the objectives and scope of the assigned research project;
- engage actively with the relevant literature, methodologies, tools, and research questions;
- work with professionalism, integrity, consistency, and collegiality;
- make steady progress toward a technically sound research outcome and a high-quality MSc dissertation;
- communicate difficulties and uncertainties early, particularly when they may affect the project timeline;
- contribute positively to the research environment of the group during the period of study.

The central objective is not simply to complete a set of technical tasks, but to demonstrate the ability to understand a research problem, apply or develop appropriate methods, evaluate the resulting evidence critically, and communicate the work clearly in the dissertation.

## General Professional Expectations

The MSc student is expected to:
- behave professionally and respectfully toward all students, staff, collaborators, and administrative personnel;
- communicate in a timely, clear, and constructive manner;
- act with honesty in research, reporting, authorship, data handling, and use of resources;
- respect deadlines, commitments, and agreed responsibilities;
- raise concerns or difficulties early rather than allowing issues to accumulate;
- represent the group and institution professionally in meetings, presentations, site visits, and other academic activities;
- comply with relevant university and laboratory rules, including those related to safety, ethics, confidentiality, and responsible conduct of research.

The student is undertaking a period of research training, but this does not reduce the expectation of professionalism. Learning and supervision should take place within a framework of responsibility, consistency, and respect for others.

## Research Performance Expectations

### Ownership and initiative

The MSc student is expected to develop increasing ownership of the assigned project during the period of study. Initially, this may involve substantial guidance from the supervisor and other researchers, particularly when learning unfamiliar methods, software, datasets, experimental procedures, or literature.

As the project progresses, the student should become increasingly capable of:
- explaining clearly the research problem, objectives, and intended contribution;
- understanding the relevant literature and existing approaches;
- planning and executing agreed research activities;
- troubleshooting technical problems systematically before relying entirely on others for solutions;
- documenting methods, experiments, decisions, and results;
- critically evaluating results rather than only generating them;
- identifying limitations and proposing reasonable improvements or next steps.

The student is encouraged to ask questions whenever necessary. However, research supervision should not be interpreted as continuous step-by-step instruction. Before requesting assistance with a technical problem, the student should normally make a reasonable effort to understand the issue, investigate possible solutions, and communicate clearly what has already been attempted.

### Quality of work

The group places strong emphasis on rigor and quality. The MSc student is expected to produce work that is technically careful, scientifically sound, reproducible where relevant, and presented to an academic standard appropriate for a master's dissertation.

This includes expectations such as:
- appropriate engagement with relevant scientific literature;
- careful implementation of experiments, numerical analyses, or computational workflows;
- appropriate validation, benchmarking, and comparison where relevant;
- clean, understandable, and reasonably documented code;
- organized management of data, models, scripts, figures, and results;
- accurate and critical interpretation of findings;
- clear and professional technical writing.

The student is not expected to possess all required skills at the beginning of the project. Nevertheless, the student is expected to take feedback seriously, learn actively, and demonstrate clear improvement throughout the research period.

### Productivity and deliverables

The precise outputs will depend on the research topic, MSc programme requirements, duration of the project, and nature of the work. The primary expected outcome is normally a completed MSc dissertation that demonstrates coherent and meaningful research.

Additional outputs may include:
- datasets, annotations, computational models, codebases, or experimental results;
- technical reports or research documentation;
- prototypes, demonstrators, or software tools;
- presentations within the group or to collaborators;
- conference abstracts, conference papers, or journal manuscripts where the quality and maturity of the work support publication.

Publication is encouraged when the research produces a contribution of sufficient quality and novelty, but it is not automatically expected from every MSc project. The dissertation and successful completion of the agreed research objectives remain the primary priorities.

Because the duration of an MSc project is relatively short, prolonged periods without tangible progress can significantly compromise the final outcome. The student is therefore expected to work consistently throughout the project rather than concentrating the majority of the work near the dissertation deadline.

### Progress across the MSc project

The precise timeline will depend on the programme and project duration, but the research should normally progress through three broad phases.

During the **initial phase**, the student is generally expected to:
- understand the project objectives, expected outputs, and available timeline;
- become familiar with the relevant literature and state of the art;
- learn the principal tools, datasets, software, or experimental procedures required for the work;
- establish, together with the supervisor, a realistic research plan and initial milestones.

During the **development phase**, the student is generally expected to:
- execute the main technical and research activities;
- progressively work with greater independence;
- evaluate results critically and refine the methodology where necessary;
- maintain organized documentation of experiments, code, data, and decisions;
- begin preparing dissertation material sufficiently early rather than postponing all writing until the end.

During the **completion phase**, the student is generally expected to:
- finalize the principal analyses and validation;
- clearly identify the conclusions, limitations, and contribution of the work;
- complete the dissertation with sufficient time for supervisory feedback and revision;
- organize and hand over all research materials generated during the project;
- discuss any potential publication or continuation of the work with the supervisor.

These phases are indicative rather than rigid. Nevertheless, because of the limited duration of an MSc project, significant delays during the initial or development phases should be communicated and addressed promptly.

## Communication, Meetings, and Responsiveness

The MSc student is expected to maintain regular communication with the supervisor and relevant team members.

Minimum expectations include:
- attending scheduled supervision meetings, group meetings, project meetings, and other relevant research discussions;
- preparing adequately for meetings, including bringing concise updates, results, questions, and proposed next steps;
- keeping a clear record of important decisions and agreed actions;
- responding to important emails and messages within a reasonable time frame;
- informing the supervisor in advance when agreed deadlines or milestones may not be met;
- communicating absences, delays, technical obstacles, or other constraints that materially affect project progress;
- sharing information in a timely way rather than working in isolation on problems that may affect the project.

Supervision meetings are most effective when the student arrives prepared and can clearly communicate:
- what has been completed since the previous discussion;
- what results or difficulties have emerged;
- what has been attempted to address those difficulties;
- what decisions or feedback are needed;
- what will be completed before the next meeting.

As a general working norm, communication should be proactive. Repeated silence, missed meetings without appropriate notice, or lack of visibility over ongoing work can substantially affect progress, particularly given the relatively short duration of an MSc project.

## Working Style, Presence, and Time Commitment

The MSc research project is outcomes-driven rather than based only on hours spent working. At the same time, successful completion requires consistent engagement throughout the research period.

The MSc student is expected to:
- maintain an appropriate and consistent level of engagement with the project and group;
- be reasonably available for scheduled meetings and collaborative work;
- allocate sufficient time to meet the agreed research milestones and dissertation requirements;
- balance research responsibilities appropriately with coursework or other formal programme requirements where applicable;
- use flexible or remote-working arrangements responsibly, where permitted;
- discuss in advance any recurring schedule constraints, extended absences, travel, or other factors that may affect progress.

The student is responsible for managing their time effectively. In particular, coursework, examinations, travel, or other foreseeable commitments should be considered when planning research activities and dissertation milestones.

## Reporting and Documentation

Good research management requires proper documentation. The MSc student is expected to maintain orderly records of relevant work, which may include:
- literature and methodological notes;
- experiment logs;
- code repositories and version histories;
- structured storage of datasets, models, scripts, figures, and results;
- summaries of important decisions and action items;
- periodic progress summaries when requested.

Research materials generated during the MSc project should be stored using the locations, repositories, or systems agreed with the group and should not exist only on personal devices or private accounts.

Good documentation is an integral part of research practice. It supports reproducibility, facilitates dissertation preparation, allows effective supervision, and ensures that the work remains understandable and usable after the student completes the project.

## Dissertation Preparation

The MSc dissertation is a central outcome of the research project and should be treated as an integral part of the research process rather than as a document to be written only after the technical work is complete.

The student is expected to:
- become familiar with the applicable MSc dissertation requirements and submission deadlines;
- develop a clear dissertation structure sufficiently early in the project;
- maintain organized literature references, figures, tables, results, and methodological descriptions throughout the research;
- begin writing relevant sections while the research is still in progress where practical;
- submit drafts or agreed sections to the supervisor sufficiently in advance to allow meaningful feedback;
- revise the dissertation carefully in response to feedback;
- take primary responsibility for the technical accuracy, clarity, completeness, formatting, and timely submission of the final dissertation.

Supervisory feedback is intended to guide the development and quality of the dissertation. It should not be understood as an obligation for the supervisor to rewrite the dissertation or correct every grammatical, formatting, or presentational issue.

The student should therefore allow sufficient time for drafting and revision. A complete dissertation provided only immediately before the institutional deadline may not allow adequate time for detailed supervisory feedback.

## Publications, Authorship, and Academic Credit

### Publication culture

The group values high-quality scholarly publication and responsible dissemination. Where an MSc project results in research of sufficient novelty, completeness, and quality, the student may be encouraged to contribute to a conference paper, journal manuscript, dataset paper, or other research output.

Publication is not automatically required for successful completion of an MSc research project. Decisions regarding publication will depend on the scientific contribution, maturity of the work, available time, and potential for further development after dissertation submission.

Where publication is pursued, the student is expected to contribute actively to manuscript preparation, revision, and submission in accordance with their contribution to the work.

### Authorship principles

Authorship should reflect substantial intellectual or technical contribution and should follow accepted academic norms as well as any relevant institutional or journal guidance. Authorship order will depend on the nature and extent of contributions and will be discussed in good faith.

General expectations include:
- authorship is earned through substantial contribution, not assumed automatically through participation in the project;
- first authorship typically requires a leading contribution to the core research and manuscript preparation;
- for publications arising primarily from the group's projects, funding, infrastructure, supervision, or intellectual direction, the supervisor must be listed as the last author, serve as the corresponding author, and be acknowledged as the principal investigator;
- for other publications connected to work conducted within the group, authorship and author order should be discussed with the supervisor at an early stage and determined based on the nature and extent of contributions;
- all listed authors are expected to contribute, review, and stand by the submitted work to a reasonable extent;
- acknowledgement may be more appropriate than authorship for limited or peripheral contributions.

The MSc student should not independently submit manuscripts, abstracts, datasets, software, or related outputs based on group work without prior discussion and approval from the supervisor and relevant co-authors.

### Continuation of publications after MSc completion

A publication arising from the MSc research may require additional analysis, validation, writing, or revision after the dissertation has been submitted or after the student's formal period in the group has ended.

Where the student wishes to remain actively involved and their contribution justifies authorship, reasonable continued participation in manuscript preparation and revision is expected. If the student is unable or unwilling to continue contributing, the remaining members of the research team may continue developing the work, with authorship determined according to the contributions made to the final publication.

### Affiliation and acknowledgement

All outputs arising from the MSc research are expected to include the correct institutional affiliation(s), required funding acknowledgements, and other sponsor- or project-specific statements where applicable.

## Data, Code, Intellectual Property, and Confidentiality

### Data and code management

Research data, code, models, annotations, figures, notes, and related materials produced as part of the MSc project must be handled responsibly and organized professionally. The MSc student is expected to follow agreed project conventions for storage, naming, backup, version control, and documentation.

### Ownership and access

Materials generated within the scope of the MSc project, especially where tied to funded projects, collaborations, group infrastructure, existing datasets, supervision, or institutional resources, should be treated as project or institutional research assets rather than purely personal assets.

The MSc student must not withhold essential files, repositories, documentation, datasets, models, or access information needed for continuity of the research.

The student's dissertation remains subject to the applicable university rules regarding academic work, intellectual property, and examination.

### Confidentiality

The MSc student may have access to unpublished results, confidential data, sponsor-sensitive information, internal proposals, software, datasets, or collaboration materials. Such information must not be disclosed, shared externally, uploaded to unauthorized services, or reused inappropriately without authorization.

### Intellectual property and external use

Any potentially patentable, commercially sensitive, confidential, or otherwise protected outputs should be discussed with the supervisor before public disclosure.

The MSc student should not independently commercialize, license, distribute, publicly release, or externally reuse project materials in ways that conflict with university policy, sponsor terms, collaboration agreements, or the legitimate interests of the group.

## Collaboration and Team Conduct

The group values a constructive, respectful, and collaborative research culture. The MSc student is expected to contribute positively to that environment.

This includes:
- interacting respectfully with students, researchers, staff, and collaborators;
- giving and receiving technical feedback professionally;
- being willing to learn from researchers with greater experience in relevant areas;
- avoiding dismissive, hostile, or inappropriate behaviour;
- recognizing that collaboration requires transparency and mutual accountability;
- supporting a culture in which members can ask questions and seek guidance without fear of disrespect;
- appropriately acknowledging assistance, resources, data, code, or intellectual contributions received from others.

The student is encouraged to participate in the broader intellectual life of the group during the research period, including relevant group meetings, research discussions, seminars, and interactions with other researchers where appropriate.

## Use of Group Resources

Access to equipment, software, computing resources, datasets, laboratory facilities, consumables, or other research resources is subject to availability, institutional rules, project scope, and supervisory approval.

The MSc student is expected to:
- use research resources responsibly and primarily for their intended academic purpose;
- follow applicable procedures for accessing laboratory facilities, computing infrastructure, datasets, or specialized equipment;
- seek approval before making purchases or incurring project-related expenses where relevant;
- handle institutional and group assets with appropriate care;
- return borrowed equipment or other resources promptly when requested or before leaving the group.

Access to a particular resource should not be assumed unless it has been explicitly agreed and is compatible with project needs and availability.

## Compliance, Ethics, and Safety

The MSc student is expected to comply with all applicable requirements related to research integrity, human subjects, data governance, safety training, laboratory or workshop rules, fieldwork procedures, and any project-specific ethical or regulatory approvals.

No research activity requiring prior approval should begin before the relevant approvals are in place.

The student should seek clarification whenever uncertain whether an activity requires ethical, safety, data-management, confidentiality, or other institutional approval.

## Communication of Concerns and Resolution of Issues

A productive research environment requires open, respectful, and timely communication in both directions. The MSc student is encouraged to raise concerns early whenever a problem, misunderstanding, or source of friction arises that may affect the research, working relationships, dissertation progress, or overall working environment.

Examples of concerns that should be communicated early include, but are not limited to:
- misunderstandings regarding responsibilities, expectations, priorities, or project scope;
- difficulties in communication or collaboration with other group members, students, staff, or external collaborators;
- concerns about authorship, acknowledgement, contribution, or publication planning;
- disagreement regarding research direction, technical decisions, or allocation of effort;
- uncertainty regarding dissertation expectations or feedback;
- obstacles affecting progress, including lack of clarity, resource limitations, technical difficulties, or personal circumstances that materially affect the work;
- any situation in which an issue may worsen if left unaddressed.

In most cases, concerns should first be raised with the supervisor in a professional and constructive manner. The purpose of such discussion is to clarify expectations, understand the issue, and identify a reasonable path forward. Where appropriate, concerns involving other members of the group or collaborators may also be addressed through a joint discussion with the relevant parties.

Concerns raised in good faith will be treated seriously and respectfully. Open communication is considered an important part of maintaining a healthy, fair, and productive research environment.

## Progress Concerns

If concerns arise regarding research progress, conduct, communication, professionalism, fulfilment of responsibilities, or the likelihood of completing the project and dissertation successfully within the available period, the usual approach will be to discuss them directly and in good faith at an early stage.

Depending on the issue, expectations and priorities may be clarified verbally or in writing, the scope of work may be reconsidered where appropriate, and reasonable steps may be agreed to improve progress.

Examples of issues that may require attention include, but are not limited to:
- persistent lack of progress without reasonable explanation;
- repeated failure to meet agreed responsibilities, milestones, or deadlines;
- poor communication or prolonged unresponsiveness;
- repeated lack of preparation for supervision meetings;
- failure to document or share essential research materials appropriately;
- postponing dissertation preparation to a point that places timely completion at risk;
- serious lapses in professional conduct or research integrity;
- behaviour that negatively affects the working environment or project continuity.

Because the MSc research period is relatively short, problems that persist for several weeks can have a substantial effect on the final outcome. Both the student and supervisor should therefore seek to identify and address significant difficulties as early as reasonably possible.

Nothing in this document limits the application of formal institutional or programme procedures where required.

## End-of-Project Responsibilities

Before completing the MSc project or leaving the group, the student is expected to support an orderly handover of the research.

This includes, as applicable:
- organizing and transferring project files, repositories, data, models, code, results, and documentation;
- ensuring that relevant files are stored in the agreed group or institutional locations;
- providing sufficient documentation for another researcher to understand and, where appropriate, reproduce or continue the work;
- summarizing the status of any unfinished analyses, technical developments, manuscripts, or other deliverables;
- returning equipment, access items, or other institutional property as required;
- clarifying any remaining publication, authorship, confidentiality, or handover matters with the supervisor.

The dissertation submission does not replace the requirement for an adequate research handover. Completion of the MSc project should not leave critical data, code, knowledge, or research materials inaccessible to the group.

## Mutual Understanding

The goal of this document is not to create rigidity, but to establish a clear professional foundation for a successful MSc research experience.

The supervisor is committed to providing appropriate academic guidance, constructive feedback, reasonable access to discussion, and support for the student's development as a researcher. The level and form of supervision may evolve as the student becomes more familiar with the research topic and gains greater independence.

In return, the MSc student is expected to engage seriously with the project, make responsible use of the available supervision and resources, communicate openly, work consistently toward the agreed objectives and dissertation, and uphold the standards outlined above.

Successful MSc research requires effort from both student and supervisor. Clear communication, realistic planning, intellectual engagement, and timely response to difficulties are therefore considered shared foundations of the supervisory relationship.
