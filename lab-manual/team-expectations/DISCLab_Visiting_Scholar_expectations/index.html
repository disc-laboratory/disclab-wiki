    Visiting Scholar Expectations and Working Principles¶

    This document outlines the main expectations, working norms, and professional responsibilities associated with a visiting scholar appointment in the Digital Intelligence for Structures and Construction research group at NTU Singapore. The term ``visiting scholar'' in this document may include, depending on the specific appointment, visiting postdoctoral researchers, visiting research fellows, visiting professors, and other experienced academic researchers hosted by the group.

    The purpose of the visit is expected to extend beyond passive academic exchange. Visiting scholars are expected to contribute actively to the research, intellectual development, mentoring, scholarly production, and broader academic activities of the group during their stay.

    Purpose of this Document¶

    This document is provided before or at the beginning of the research visit to clarify expectations and support an effective, productive, and mutually beneficial collaboration. Its purpose is to reduce ambiguity, support early alignment, and establish a common understanding of the level of engagement expected from a senior visiting researcher.

    This document is not an employment contract and does not replace university, school, funding-agency, immigration, human resources, safety, ethics, intellectual-property, or other institutional rules applicable to the visit. Official NTU policies, the rules of the scholar's home institution, and the formal terms of the visiting appointment remain controlling where applicable.

    Instead, this document sets out the research-group-level expectations associated with being hosted as a visiting scholar in DISCLab.

    Nature of the Appointment¶

    A visiting scholar appointment is a research-focused, time-bounded academic engagement intended to generate meaningful scientific exchange and identifiable scholarly outcomes. Unlike a visiting student appointment, the visiting scholar is expected to enter the group with substantial research experience and to operate with a high degree of intellectual and technical independence.

    The visiting scholar is expected to: - contribute actively to the research objectives associated with the visit and, where relevant, to the broader research agenda of the group; - integrate efficiently into the scientific and technical activities of the lab; - work with professionalism, integrity, consistency, and collegiality; - operate with substantial independence appropriate to an experienced researcher; - initiate, develop, and help complete high-quality scholarly outputs; - contribute intellectually to ongoing and emerging research directions; - support the development of graduate and undergraduate researchers where appropriate; - contribute actively to proposal development, research planning, and collaboration-building where relevant; - help strengthen the academic network, visibility, and research capacity of the group.

    A visiting scholar is not expected to function merely as an observer or temporary affiliate. The appointment should result in a clear and traceable contribution to the scientific activity of the group.

    General Professional Expectations¶

    The visiting scholar is expected to: - behave professionally and respectfully toward students, staff, collaborators, and administrative personnel; - communicate in a timely, clear, and constructive manner; - act with honesty in research, reporting, authorship, data handling, and use of resources; - respect deadlines, commitments, and agreed responsibilities; - raise concerns or disagreements early rather than allowing issues to accumulate; - represent the group and institution professionally in meetings, presentations, conferences, site visits, and online communication; - comply with relevant university and laboratory rules, including those related to safety, ethics, confidentiality, and responsible conduct of research.

    The seniority of the visiting scholar is expected to be reflected not only in research independence but also in professional judgement, reliability, collegial behaviour, and constructive engagement with junior researchers.

    Research Performance Expectations¶

    Research ownership and independence¶

    The visiting scholar is expected to take substantial ownership of the research activities associated with the visit. Guidance and scientific discussion will be available, but the scholar should normally be capable of defining technical steps, evaluating alternatives, solving problems independently, and converting discussions into concrete research progress.

    The visiting scholar should be able to: - understand and contribute to the scientific goals, technical scope, and intended outputs of the visit; - plan and execute research activities with a high degree of independence; - identify technical or scientific bottlenecks and propose viable solutions; - critically assess results, assumptions, limitations, and methodological choices; - propose new research directions, extensions, or collaborations where appropriate; - connect the work conducted during the visit to broader scientific questions and opportunities; - contribute intellectual leadership within areas aligned with the scholar's expertise.

    The expectation is not that all work must be conducted individually. Collaboration is strongly encouraged. However, the visiting scholar should contribute as an experienced researcher rather than requiring continuous task-level direction.

    Quality and rigor of work¶

    The group places strong emphasis on scientific rigor and quality. Work carried out during the visit should be technically careful, scientifically sound, reproducible where relevant, and presented to a professional academic standard.

    This includes expectations such as: - well-founded research design and methodological choices; - appropriate validation, benchmarking, and uncertainty awareness; - clean, understandable, and documented computational workflows where relevant; - organized data and version-control practices; - critical interpretation of results rather than purely descriptive reporting; - professional writing for manuscripts, reports, proposals, presentations, and other research outputs; - appropriate consideration of reproducibility, limitations, and scientific transparency.

    Academic productivity and deliverables¶

    A visiting scholar is expected to contribute consistently to tangible academic outputs during the visit. The precise form and number of outputs will depend on the duration of the stay, the maturity of the underlying work, the scholar's expertise, and the nature of the agreed collaboration.

    Relevant outputs may include: - journal manuscripts; - conference papers or abstracts; - review, perspective, or methodological papers; - research proposals and funding applications; - technical reports, datasets, software, codebases, prototypes, or demonstrators; - research frameworks, methodological developments, or validation studies; - presentations, seminars, workshops, or invited research discussions; - structured research plans that lead to continued collaboration after the visit.

    For visits of sufficient duration, the expectation is that scholarly production should be continuous rather than incidental. The scholar should therefore work toward identifiable outputs throughout the visit rather than treating publication or proposal development as optional activities considered only at the end.

    The precise expected outputs should be discussed early and adjusted reasonably according to the duration and circumstances of the visit.

    Progress across the visit¶

    Although the exact trajectory depends on the length and purpose of the visit, the appointment should normally progress through three broad stages.

    During the initial stage, the visiting scholar is generally expected to: - become familiar quickly with the relevant project context, datasets, tools, collaborators, and ongoing research; - clarify the concrete objectives, intended outputs, and priorities of the visit; - identify opportunities where the scholar's expertise can add immediate value; - establish a realistic plan for research, publications, mentoring, and proposal contributions.

    During the main stage, the visiting scholar is generally expected to: - contribute actively to research development, experimentation, analysis, or methodological work; - produce intermediate and final scholarly outputs; - contribute to manuscript preparation and proposal development; - participate in mentoring and technical discussion with students and researchers; - identify opportunities for collaboration that may extend beyond the visit; - communicate clearly about progress, challenges, and next steps.

    During the final stage, the visiting scholar is generally expected to: - consolidate the principal outputs of the visit; - advance manuscripts and proposals to a sufficiently mature state; - document and hand over relevant research materials; - clarify responsibilities for work that will continue after departure; - establish, where appropriate, a concrete plan for continued collaboration.

    These stages are indicative rather than rigid. The central expectation is that the visit demonstrates a credible progression from rapid integration to substantive contribution and sustainable academic outcomes.

    Communication, Meetings, and Responsiveness¶

    The visiting scholar is expected to maintain regular and proactive communication with the host supervisor and relevant group members.

    Minimum expectations include: - attending scheduled research meetings, group meetings, project meetings, and other relevant academic discussions; - preparing adequately for meetings and communicating substantive progress, questions, and next steps; - responding to important emails and messages within a reasonable time frame; - informing the host supervisor in advance when agreed deadlines may not be met; - communicating absences, delays, obstacles, or external commitments that materially affect progress; - sharing relevant information in a timely way rather than allowing parallel or disconnected work to develop unnecessarily.

    A senior visiting researcher is expected to contribute actively to scientific discussion. This includes challenging assumptions constructively, sharing expertise, proposing solutions, and helping the group make informed research decisions.

    Working Style, Presence, and Time Commitment¶

    The visiting scholar appointment is outcomes-driven rather than based solely on physical presence or fixed working hours. At the same time, the scholar is expected to demonstrate a professional level of engagement, availability, and participation commensurate with the purpose of the visit.

    The visiting scholar is expected to: - maintain an appropriate and consistent level of engagement with the group; - be reasonably available during normal working periods for meetings, mentoring, and collaborative research; - allocate sufficient time to achieve the agreed research and academic outputs; - use flexible or remote-working arrangements responsibly, where permitted; - discuss in advance recurring schedule constraints, extended travel, significant external commitments, or other factors that may affect the visit.

    The appointment should not be treated as a passive affiliation, office-hosting arrangement, or primarily personal research stay disconnected from the host group. The scholar is expected to engage meaningfully with DISCLab and contribute to its academic activity during the visit.

    Reporting and Documentation¶

    Good research management requires proper documentation. The visiting scholar is expected to maintain orderly records of relevant work, which may include: - experiment logs and methodological notes; - code repositories and version histories; - structured storage of datasets, models, scripts, figures, and reports; - manuscript and proposal drafts; - summaries of important decisions and action items; - progress updates suitable for internal tracking or sponsor reporting where relevant.

    If requested, the visiting scholar should provide concise periodic updates on progress, obstacles, priorities, and next steps. Research materials generated during the visit should be stored in a manner accessible to the group and not solely on personal devices or private accounts.

    Documentation is particularly important because the scholar's formal stay may end while manuscripts, proposals, or collaborative projects continue.

    Mentoring and Academic Contribution to the Group¶

    A visiting scholar is expected to contribute not only through individual research but also through the development of the broader research environment.

    Depending on expertise and project needs, this may include: - providing technical or scientific guidance to PhD and MSc students; - supporting undergraduate research projects, final-year projects, or research assistants where appropriate; - reviewing manuscripts, presentations, code, or experimental plans; - helping students identify methodological weaknesses and improve research rigor; - participating in research brainstorming, internal seminars, journal discussions, or technical workshops; - sharing specialist knowledge, tools, datasets, workflows, or research practices with the group; - contributing to the intellectual development of junior researchers without replacing their formal supervisors.

    The nature and intensity of mentoring should be proportionate to the duration of the visit and the scholar's expertise. However, some contribution to the development of junior researchers is generally expected from senior visitors.

    Mentoring should be constructive, respectful, and aligned with the research objectives agreed with the student's formal supervisor.

    Research Proposals and Funding Development¶

    Visiting scholars are expected, where relevant to the duration and scope of the visit, to contribute actively to the development of future research opportunities.

    This may include: - identifying suitable national, international, or institutional funding opportunities; - contributing to the scientific framing and research questions of proposals; - preparing or reviewing technical sections, work packages, methodologies, impact statements, or research plans; - helping identify appropriate collaborators and complementary expertise; - contributing preliminary results, figures, literature reviews, or supporting material; - participating in meetings related to proposal preparation; - helping establish follow-on collaborations that may support joint funding after the visit.

    The level of contribution will depend on eligibility, funding rules, visit duration, and the scholar's role in the proposed project. Nevertheless, proposal development is considered an important component of senior academic collaboration and should be approached as a substantive scholarly activity rather than only an administrative task.

    Where a proposal builds significantly on work, intellectual direction, preliminary results, infrastructure, or collaborations developed within DISCLab, roles and responsibilities should be discussed transparently at an early stage.

    Publications, Authorship, and Academic Credit¶

    Publication culture¶

    The group values high-quality scholarly publication and responsible dissemination. The visiting scholar is expected to contribute actively and consistently to manuscript development when the research undertaken during the visit supports publication.

    Because publication cycles often extend beyond the duration of a visit, a manuscript need not necessarily be published before departure. However, work intended for publication should normally be left in a sufficiently mature, documented, and structured form to support efficient continuation after the visit.

    Authorship principles¶

    Authorship should reflect substantial intellectual or technical contribution and should follow accepted academic norms as well as relevant institutional and journal guidance. Authorship order should be discussed in good faith and should reflect the nature and extent of the contributions made.

    General expectations include: - authorship is earned through substantial contribution and is not assumed solely on the basis of title, seniority, affiliation, or presence during the visit; - first authorship typically requires a leading contribution to the core research and manuscript preparation; - for publications arising primarily from the group's projects, funding, infrastructure, supervision, or intellectual direction, the DISCLab supervisor must be listed as the last author, serve as the corresponding author, and be acknowledged as the principal investigator; - for collaborative publications that draw substantially on work conducted within DISCLab, authorship and author order should be discussed with the host supervisor at an early stage and determined based on the nature and extent of contributions; - contributions from the visiting scholar's home institution and home collaborators should be recognized appropriately and discussed transparently; - all listed authors are expected to contribute, review, and stand by the submitted work to a reasonable extent; - acknowledgement may be more appropriate than authorship for limited or peripheral contributions.

    The visiting scholar should not independently submit manuscripts, abstracts, datasets, software, or related outputs based substantially on DISCLab work without prior discussion and approval from the host supervisor and relevant co-authors.

    Continuation after the visit¶

    Publications and research activities initiated during the visit may continue after the scholar has returned to the home institution. Where the scholar wishes to remain actively involved and their contribution justifies authorship, reasonable continued participation in manuscript development, revision, and response to reviewers is expected.

    Likewise, the group may continue developing work initiated during the visit where additional research is required. Authorship should reflect the contributions ultimately made to the completed work.

    Affiliation and acknowledgement¶

    All outputs arising from the visit are expected to include appropriate institutional affiliation(s), funding acknowledgements, and project-specific statements where applicable.

    Where the work substantially involves both the home and host institutions, the appropriate use and order of affiliations should be discussed and should reflect the actual context of the research and applicable institutional policies.

    Data, Code, Intellectual Property, and Confidentiality¶

    Data and code management¶

    Research data, code, models, annotations, figures, notes, and related materials produced or substantially developed during the visit must be handled responsibly and organized professionally. The visiting scholar is expected to follow agreed project conventions for storage, naming, backup, version control, and documentation.

    Ownership and access¶

    Materials generated within the scope of the visit, especially where tied to funded projects, collaborations, group infrastructure, datasets, supervision, or institutional resources, should be treated as project or institutional research assets rather than purely personal assets.

    The visiting scholar must not withhold essential files, repositories, documentation, models, datasets, or access information required for continuity of the research.

    The visiting scholar's pre-existing intellectual property, data, code, or research materials remain subject to the applicable policies and agreements of the scholar's home institution and any relevant collaboration arrangements.

    Confidentiality¶

    The visiting scholar may have access to unpublished results, confidential data, sponsor-sensitive information, internal proposals, software, peer-review material, student work, or collaboration information. Such information must not be disclosed, shared externally, uploaded to unauthorized services, or reused inappropriately without authorization.

    This expectation applies equally to communications with the scholar's home institution or external collaborators unless sharing has been agreed and is permitted under the relevant arrangements.

    Intellectual property and external use¶

    Any potentially patentable, commercially sensitive, confidential, or otherwise protected outputs should be discussed before public disclosure.

    The visiting scholar should not independently commercialize, license, distribute, publicly release, or externally reuse project materials in ways that conflict with university policy, sponsor terms, collaboration agreements, or the legitimate interests of the group.

    Where questions arise regarding subsequent use of research materials after the visit, these should be discussed explicitly rather than assumed.

    Collaboration and Team Conduct¶

    The group values a constructive, respectful, and collaborative research culture. The visiting scholar is expected to contribute positively to that environment and, as a senior researcher, to model appropriate academic conduct.

    This includes: - interacting respectfully with students, researchers, staff, and collaborators; - giving and receiving technically sound and professional feedback; - avoiding dismissive, hostile, intimidating, or otherwise inappropriate behaviour; - recognizing that collaboration requires transparency, communication, and mutual accountability; - supporting an environment in which junior researchers can ask questions and discuss mistakes constructively; - recognizing appropriately the contributions of students and other collaborators; - avoiding inappropriate interference with existing supervisory relationships or project responsibilities.

    The scholar is encouraged to contribute intellectually across the group while respecting existing project structures, responsibilities, and supervisory arrangements.

    Use of Group Resources¶

    Access to equipment, software, computing resources, datasets, laboratory facilities, consumables, travel support, or other research resources is subject to availability, institutional rules, project scope, and approval where required.

    The visiting scholar is expected to: - use research resources responsibly and for their intended academic purpose; - follow applicable procedures for accessing laboratory facilities, computing infrastructure, datasets, and specialized equipment; - seek approval before making purchases or incurring project-related expenses where required; - understand that not all conference, travel, procurement, or resource requests can necessarily be supported; - handle institutional and group assets with appropriate care; - return borrowed equipment or other resources before leaving the group.

    Compliance, Ethics, and Safety¶

    The visiting scholar is expected to comply with all applicable requirements related to research integrity, human subjects, data governance, safety training, laboratory or workshop rules, fieldwork procedures, and project-specific ethical or regulatory approvals.

    No research activity requiring prior approval should begin before the relevant approvals are in place.

    The scholar's seniority or affiliation with another institution does not reduce the obligation to comply with host-institution requirements while conducting research at NTU.

    Communication of Concerns and Resolution of Issues¶

    A productive research environment requires open, respectful, and timely communication in both directions. The visiting scholar is encouraged to raise concerns early whenever a problem, misunderstanding, or source of friction arises that may affect the research, working relationships, academic credit, or overall working environment.

    Examples of concerns that should be communicated early include, but are not limited to: - misunderstandings regarding responsibilities, expectations, priorities, or project scope; - difficulties in communication or collaboration with group members, students, staff, home-institution colleagues, or external collaborators; - concerns about authorship, acknowledgement, contribution, publication planning, or intellectual property; - disagreement regarding research direction, technical decisions, use of data, or allocation of effort; - concerns regarding mentoring roles or interaction with students; - obstacles affecting progress, including lack of clarity, resource limitations, or external commitments that materially affect the work; - any situation in which an issue may worsen if left unaddressed.

    In most cases, concerns should first be raised with the host supervisor in a professional and constructive manner. The purpose of such discussion is to clarify expectations, understand the perspectives involved, and identify a reasonable path forward.

    Where appropriate, concerns involving other members of the group, the scholar's home institution, or external collaborators may be addressed through a joint discussion with the relevant parties.

    Concerns raised in good faith will be treated seriously and respectfully. Open communication is considered an important part of maintaining a healthy, fair, and productive academic environment.

    Performance and Engagement Concerns¶

    If concerns arise regarding research contribution, professional conduct, communication, mentoring, fulfilment of agreed responsibilities, or meaningful engagement during the visit, the usual approach will be to discuss them directly and in good faith at an early stage.

    Depending on the issue, expectations may be clarified verbally or in writing and, where appropriate, priorities or responsibilities may be adjusted.

    Examples of issues that may require attention include, but are not limited to: - persistent lack of meaningful research progress without reasonable explanation; - repeated failure to meet agreed responsibilities or deadlines; - poor communication or prolonged unresponsiveness; - limited engagement with the group relative to the stated purpose of the visit; - failure to document or share essential research materials appropriately; - repeated lack of contribution to agreed scholarly outputs; - inappropriate conduct toward students, colleagues, staff, or collaborators; - serious lapses in professional conduct or research integrity; - behaviour that negatively affects the working environment, project continuity, or collaborative relationships.

    Nothing in this document limits the application of formal institutional procedures or the terms governing the visiting appointment where required.

    End-of-Visit Responsibilities¶

    Before the visit ends, the visiting scholar is expected to support an orderly transition and ensure that the work undertaken remains useful and accessible.

    This includes, as applicable: - organizing and transferring project files, repositories, data, models, code, and documentation; - summarizing the status of ongoing research tasks, manuscripts, proposals, and other deliverables; - documenting outstanding technical issues and recommended next steps; - returning equipment, access items, or other institutional property as required; - clarifying publication, authorship, proposal, intellectual-property, confidentiality, or handover matters; - identifying responsibilities and timelines for work that will continue after departure; - helping ensure that collaborations initiated during the visit have a clear path for continuation where appropriate.

    Completion of the visit should not leave important research materials, manuscripts, proposals, or knowledge dependent on inaccessible personal files or informal arrangements.

    A successful visit should leave behind a meaningful scientific contribution and, where appropriate, a stronger foundation for continued collaboration between DISCLab and the scholar or the scholar's home institution.

    Mutual Understanding¶

    The goal of this document is not to create rigidity, but to establish a clear professional foundation for an effective and mutually beneficial academic visit.

    The host supervisor and group are committed to providing an intellectually engaging research environment, reasonable access to discussion, opportunities for collaboration, appropriate integration into relevant research activities, and support for productive scientific exchange.

    In return, the visiting scholar is expected to engage seriously with the appointment, contribute at a level appropriate to an experienced researcher, support the development of junior researchers where relevant, contribute consistently to scholarly outputs and research proposals, communicate openly, and uphold the standards outlined above.

    The value of a visiting scholar appointment should therefore be measured not only by the work completed during the physical stay, but also by the research capacity, scholarly outputs, collaborations, and opportunities that the visit helps create.
